#### Should I Take This Course?

<a id="am-i-or-my-child-too-young-to-take-this-course"></a>

##### Am I or my child too young to take this course?

Our courses are perhaps best suited for learners ages 12 and up. Younger learners might need a hand from a parent. Please note that your local laws and policies may prevent learners under certain ages from utilizing free third-party services associated with this course. Accordingly, younger learners may need your assistance in signing up for these free services.

<a id="does-this-course-have-prerequisites"></a>

##### Does this course have prerequisites?

While you are not required to take or show proof that you have passed a previous course, it is highly recommended that you examine our [course map](https://cs50.harvard.edu/x/faqs/#which-course-should-i-take) to determine if this class is the right one for you at this point in your development as a learner.

<a id="can-this-course-be-used-to-fulfill-official-academic-requirements-for-my-university-college-or-school"></a>

##### Can this course be used to fulfill official academic requirements for my university, college, or school?

Our free and verified certificates are not accredited academic offerings from Harvard or any of its affiliate institutions. We offer these certificates for self-educational and vocational purposes.

Schools and universities throughout the world have varying rules about what specifically they will accept for academic transfer credit. Accordingly, you should speak to your specific academic advisors about your individual situation. It’s possible (though you will have to verify with your advisors) that our certificate will satisfy their requirements. Indeed, some of our courses (facilitated through edX) are recommended by the [American Council on Education (ACE)](https://cs50.harvard.edu/x/faqs/#how-can-i-take-advantage-of-american-council-on-education-ace-credit-recommendation) for college or university credit, which your own academic institution may honor.

If you are looking for an accredited academic course or certificate at the undergraduate or graduate level, consider exploring [Harvard Extension](https://extension.harvard.edu).

If you are a high school student looking for academic credit, best to show your teacher or academic counselor [CS50 AP](https://cs50.harvard.edu/ap). You will need to obtain their authorization to gain official academic credit. You are very welcome to take this or any of our courses. However, only your official school representatives can provide you with academic credit for completing any of our courses.

<a id="how-can-i-take-advantage-of-american-council-on-education-ace-credit-recommendation"></a>

##### How can I take advantage of American Council on Education (ACE) credit recommendation?

[ACE](https://acenet.edu), in the words of their own website, helps learners seeking “to jump-start [their] college careers with transfer credit, or open up new employment opportunities with badges.” Further, they state “the ACE National Guide [can be used] to find courses and exams that carry college credit or competency recommendations.”

Some of our courses, facilitated through edX’s verified certificate program, are included in the [ACE National Guide](https://www.acenet.edu/National-Guide/). As of the last revision of these FAQs, our courses that appear in the ACE National Guide include:

* [CS50x: Introduction to Computer Science](https://www.acenet.edu/National-Guide/Pages/Course.aspx?org=EdX&cid=57473b93-f36c-ee11-8df0-00224805f697&oid=bb0ea623-cb38-eb11-a813-000d3a33232a);
* [CS50 AI: Introduction to Artificial Intelligence with Python](https://www.acenet.edu/National-Guide/Pages/Course.aspx?org=EdX&cid=70589b18-6473-ee11-9ae7-00224805f697&oid=bb0ea623-cb38-eb11-a813-000d3a33232a); and
* [CS50 Web: Web Programming with Python and JavaScript](https://www.acenet.edu/National-Guide/Pages/Course.aspx?org=EdX&cid=8598f7dc-136d-ee11-8df0-00224805fa3a&oid=bb0ea623-cb38-eb11-a813-000d3a33232a).

Your college or university, present or future, may accept our courses that are included in the ACE National Guide and for which you have earned an edX verified certificate. However, it is highly recommended that you check with your school in advance of taking our courses if you wish for them to be accepted for official academic credit. Free CS50 Certificates cannot be used to fulfill ACE’s requirements.

The process for obtaining such credit is as follows:

1. Select one of our courses from the ACE National Guide (see above).
2. Verify with your academic institution whether or not they will accept this course, listed in the ACE National Guide, for academic credit.
3. Enroll in the edX verified track for the course and make payment. You can do so utilizing one of the following links:
   * [CS50x](https://cs50.edx.org)
   * [CS50 AI](https://cs50.edx.org/ai)
   * [CS50 Web](https://cs50.edx.org/web)
4. Earn and receive your edX verified certificate for the course.
5. Obtain an official ACE credit note or ACE transcript from edX. Sometimes, the edX verified certificate alone may be sufficient for ACE documentation, provided the verified certificate explicitly includes an ACE credit recommendation.
6. Claim your ACE Credit Badge on Credly. edX will typically provide you with a link or invitation to claim your Credly badge when they issue your edX verified certificate.
7. Retain your edX verified certificate, your ACE documentation (such as credit note or transcript), and your Credly badge.
8. When appropriate, present these forms of documentation of completion to your academic institution.

If you previously completed the course during the enrollment period listed on the ACE National Guide website and obtain a verified certificate, you will be able to retroactively obtain the above. However, the course completion must have taken place during the enrollment period, which changes from time to time. If you wish to upgrade to an edX verified certificate after you have completed the course, after you have verified your enrollment period was during the period stated by the ACE National Guide (see above), you can see our [certificate upgrade instructions](https://cs50.harvard.edu/x/faqs/#if-i-paid-for-a-verified-certificate-after-i-completed-the-course-do-i-need-to-tell-you).

All inquiries about ACE credit should be directed to edX support.

##### Can I obtain a letter confirming my enrollment and details of the course?

We cannot provide a personalized email detailing your enrollment in this course. <a id="if-i-want-an-edx-verified-certificate-in-the-future-do-i-have-to-pay-for-verification-before-starting-the-course"></a>

##### If I want an edX verified certificate in the future, do I have to pay for verification before starting the course?

Nope! If you would like a verified certificate from edX, you can pay for verification at any point up until 10 days before the course’s final deadline, even after completing all of the coursework. However, we strongly recommend, to avoid any logistical issues that can delay certificate issuance, that you verify before finishing the course. Otherwise, the 30-day wait we reference [below](https://cs50.harvard.edu/x/faqs/#how-do-i-get-my-verified-certificate-from-edx) only starts once you have had your identity verified by edX. Thus, waiting to register for a verified certificate after you complete the course will likely delay receiving your verified certificate for 30 days or more.

<a id="if-i-paid-for-a-verified-certificate-after-i-completed-the-course-do-i-need-to-tell-you"></a>

##### If I paid for a verified certificate after I completed the course, do I need to tell you?

If you completed the course during this calendar year: No. As long as your [Gradebook](https://cs50.me/cs50x) shows that you have completed the course, you will receive your certificate up to 30 days after you make payment for a verified certificate with edX.

If you completed the course prior to this calendar year: Yes. Send us a copy of your CS50 Certificate, your edX and GitHub usernames that you used to complete the course, and a receipt from your purchase of the edX verified certificate. You must purchase the edX verified certificate using the same edX account you used to complete the course prior.

<a id="can-i-get-a-verified-certificate-for-a-cs50-course-i-completed-prior-to-this-year"></a>

##### Can I get a verified certificate for a CS50 course I completed prior to this year?

Yes, we can provide a verified certificate for any course completion from 2019 to present. We do so provided that you can send us a copy of your CS50 Certificate, your edX and GitHub usernames that you used to complete the course, and a receipt from your purchase of the edX verified certificate. Without this information, we cannot provide such a certificate.

<a id="if-i-paid-for-a-verified-certificate-before-this-calendar-year-do-i-need-to-pay-again"></a>

##### If I paid for a verified certificate before this calendar year, do I need to pay again?

No, your verification should be good for an entire year, but if it has expired, you should be able to re-verify yourself via edX’s [dashboard](https://courses.edx.org/dashboard).

<a id="tracking-your-progress"></a>

#### Tracking Your Progress

<a id="how-do-i-know-if-i-have-completed-all-requirements-for-a-certificate"></a>

##### How do I know if I have completed all requirements for a certificate?

Your [Gradebook](https://cs50.me/cs50x) shows the result for each assignment submission for the most current version of the course. The top of your [Gradebook](https://cs50.me/cs50x) shows your current status in the course, showing the number of required assignments you have completed. If your [Gradebook](https://cs50.me/cs50x) states that you are not enrolled in the course, best to enroll and start completing the required assignments.

Your [Gradebook](https://cs50.me/cs50x) groups assignments by week. Once you have received a score of 70% or higher on every required assignment in a given week, that week’s number will be shown in green, and your progress indicator will be updated to reflect that.

Once you have scored at least a 70% on all required assignments, you will see a green banner atop that page, confirming your completion and providing you next steps to obtain your certificate(s).

<a id="what--where-is-the-gradebook-for-this-course"></a>

##### What / where is the Gradebook for this course?

CS50x uses our own [Gradebook](https://cs50.me/cs50x). If you’ve never visited that link before, you’ll need to click the link to “enroll,” which really just enables your [Gradebook](https://cs50.me/cs50x) access! Registration with edX, whether for a free or verified certificate, is required.

<a id="why-does-the-gradebook-say-that-i-am-not-enrolled"></a>

##### Why does the [Gradebook](https://cs50.me/cs50x) say that I am not enrolled?

There are many potential reasons for this. The most common reasons are as follows. First, it’s possible that you never enrolled in the course before. Second, it’s possible that you never linked your edX account to your gradebook. Best to follow the on-screen instructions in your [Gradebook](https://cs50.me/cs50x) to resolve this issue and enroll.

<a id="does-this-course-have-grades"></a>

##### Does this course have grades?

No. This course has numeric scores. An assignment that receives a score of 70% or higher is considered passing.

<a id="does-my-gradebook-only-show-if-i-scored-high-enough-to-pass-a-problem--how-can-i-see-my-actual-numeric-grades"></a>

##### Does my Gradebook only show if I scored high enough to pass a problem? How can I see my actual numeric grades?

No. Your [Gradebook](https://cs50.me/cs50x) shows a green check next to each problem you passed and a red “x” next to each problem you submitted but did not pass. To see your score, hover over the red “x”, or click the problem’s name to open that submission on submit.cs50.io.

Your overall problem score is weighted 75% for correctness and 25% for style. In order to receive the green tick indicating completion, your overall score must be 70% or higher. As an example, if your check50 score is 7/8 and your style score is 0.95, your overall score will be (7/8 * 0.75) + (0.95 * 0.25) = 89%. The submission page shows your individual check50 and style50 scores.

<a id="i-completed-an-assignment-or-this-course-in-a-prior-year-why-does-my-gradebook-no-longer-show-my-prior-progress"></a>

##### I completed an assignment or this course in a prior year. Why does my Gradebook no longer show my prior progress?

At the start of each calendar year, we clean up our [Gradebook](https://cs50.me/cs50x) and “reset” it. This reset follows the calendar year, not the course’s terms, so each January it removes work from before the previous calendar year, even if the course has been extended.

* If you submitted an assignment in 2025, that work carries over to your [Gradebook](https://cs50.me/cs50x) automatically.
* If you last submitted an assignment before 2025, your progress will not carry over, and you’ll need to start the course again.

<a id="why-does-edx-think-my-grade-is-0"></a>

##### Why does edX think my grade is 0%?

This course does not use edX’s built-in gradebook, so all students’ scores always appear (in edX’s gradebook) as 0%. Not to worry, see your [Gradebook](https://cs50.me/cs50x) for your actual results. If you recently completed the course, it can take up to 30 days for edX to acknowledge your completion on your edX dashboard.

<a id="i-watched-all-the-videos-in-edx-or-on-youtube-why-dont-i-have-a-certificate"></a>

##### I watched all the videos in edX or on YouTube: Why don’t I have a certificate?

This course has numerous assignments that must be completed in addition to watching the course lectures. Best to see the course menu, which lists the various assignments for the course.

<a id="why-does-my-edx-dashboard-say-i-can-resume-the-course-even-though-i-have-completed-it"></a>

##### Why does my edX dashboard say I can “resume” the course even though I have completed it?

Your edX dashboard may display either `View Course` or `Resume Course` as a convenience to you, such that you can return to or view the course. It doesn’t indicate completion or non-completion.

<a id="github-has-disabled-my-account-what-do-i-do"></a>

##### GitHub has disabled my account. What do I do?

Best to consult GitHub’s [documentation](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/acceptable-use-policies/github-appeal-and-reinstatement) regarding account reinstatement. We cannot transfer your past progress to a new GitHub account. Further, we cannot access your files from a disabled GitHub account. If you cannot regain access to your GitHub account, you will need to restart the course.

<a id="problem-sets-assignments-and-assessments"></a>

#### Problem Sets, Assignments, and Assessments

<a id="does-this-course-have-a-test"></a>

##### Does this course have a test?

This course does not have a summative test or “final exam.” While our courses at Harvard College and Harvard Extension often have final tests, this course does not. You need only complete the assignments listed in each week or module of the course.

<a id="whats-the-difference-between-less-comfortable-and-more-comfortable-problems-do-i-have-to-do-both"></a>

##### What’s the difference between “less comfortable” and “more comfortable” problems? Do I have to do both?

In some earlier problem sets, you’ll have a choice between a “less comfortable” and a “more comfortable” problem.

The “less comfortable” problems are what you might consider the “standard” version of the problem, designed for students who have little or no prior experience. The “more comfortable” problems are the “challenge” version, designed for students who consider themselves more comfortable due to prior study/experience before this class. As such, they may require more concepts than have been covered in the course so far.

If you find yourself really struggling with the “more comfortable” version, you should switch to the “less” version (or if you’ve already completed that, simply move on and perhaps revisit in a later week). It would be better for you to skip the problem than to become so frustrated as to drop out of the course entirely. For reference, in the Fall 2019 Harvard class, only 20-30% of students submitted the “more comfortable” problems.

You don’t get any extra points for doing the “more comfortable” problems. If you submit both, the one with the highest grade will count towards your overall course progress.

<a id="did-you-get-my-submission"></a>

##### Did you get my submission?

Once you execute the `submit50` command with the appropriate *slug* as instructed by the assignment specification, your [Gradebook](https://cs50.me/cs50x) will be immediately updated with your passing status, provided that your submission was passing! Before using `submit50`, best to use `check50` to make sure you have completed the entire problem before submission.

<a id="i-submitted-a-project-but-nothing-is-showing-up-in-my-gradebook-yet-showing-that-ive-submitted-why"></a>

##### I submitted a project, but nothing is showing up in my Gradebook yet showing that I’ve submitted. Why?

Provided that you have executed the `submit50` command with the appropriate slug as instructed by the assignment specification, your passing status should immediately be posted to your [Gradebook](https://cs50.me/cs50x). First, best to run `check50` on your assignment to ensure it fulfills the requirements. Second, if that still does not work, ensure that the GitHub username you are using to log into [CS50.dev](https://cs50.dev) is the same GitHub username that appears at the top of your [Gradebook](https://cs50.me/cs50x).

<a id="if-i-submitted-a-problem-and-did-not-reach-the-70-passing-grade-can-i-resubmit"></a>

##### If I submitted a problem and did not reach the 70% passing grade, can I resubmit?

Yes! You may resubmit; we will only count your most recent submission towards your progress.

<a id="if-my-check50-score-is-not-as-expected-why-might-that-be"></a>

##### If my check50 score is not as expected, why might that be?

Your check50 score is accurate. After running check50, note that a URL will be provided linking you to your results. Note that check50 will look for precise fulfillment of the checks. Hence, if it is expecting a line break after an output and no such line break exists, the check will show as unfulfilled. In some cases, the expected and actual output may look exactly the same in the check50 results. In such an instance, best to consider how spaces and other non-visible characters may be at play. Of course, it’s also possible that you have misunderstood a portion of the assignment and need to re-read the problem set specification.

<a id="if-my-style-score-is-not-as-expected-why-might-that-be"></a>

##### If my style score is not as expected, why might that be?

Your style grade will be based on the style50 results for every source code file in your submission. When you run submit50, be sure that your folder includes only those files that are necessary for the problem set. If you have any extra test files in there, those will be counted towards your grade. Remove the extra files from the folder and resubmit to get an updated style score.

<a id="why-did-i-not-pass-my-final-project"></a>

##### Why did I not pass my final project?

After submitting your final project, you can view your submission in your [Gradebook](https://cs50.me/cs50x) to learn details about your score and why you did not pass.

The most common reason for not receiving a passing score on your final project is that you have not followed the [Final Project Specification](https://cs50.harvard.edu/x/project/).

The second most common reason for not receiving a passing score on your final project relates to your documentation (your final project’s README or DESIGN file). When your README or DESIGN documentation does not fulfill the stated requirements, you will not pass the final project. The documentation file should be quite robust. Students that don’t complete a robust documentation file, including MANY paragraphs, will not receive a passing grade. You are highly advised to create a robust documentation file, including the precise sections mentioned by the [Final Project Specification](https://cs50.harvard.edu/x/project/). Further, note that the [Final Project Specification](https://cs50.harvard.edu/x/project/) includes a rough word count for your documentation. Following these specifications will ensure that your documentation file includes all the details required for you to be able to pass.

##### If I did 70% of the problems, do I get a certificate?

No. You must do all the required assignments and receive at least a score of 70% on each.

<a id="deadlines"></a>

#### Deadlines

<a id="when-are-the-deadlines"></a>

##### When are the deadlines?

This course does not have individual deadlines for assignments. You are welcome to work on and submit at your own pace.

The overall deadline for the course is currently end-of-day [UTC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinated_Universal_Time), sometimes referred to as Zulu Time, on 30 June 2027 at 11:59PM. For your convenience, we will often render this time in your local timezone ([2027-06-30T23:59:00+00:00](https://time.cs50.io/20270630T235900Z)).

If all required work is turned in before the above time and ultimately receives a satisfactory grade (whether before or shortly after that deadline), you will be considered to have completed the course.

<a id="if-i-dont-finish-the-course-before-end-of-day-30-june-2027-what-will-happen"></a>

##### If I don’t finish the course before end-of-day 30 June 2027, what will happen?

At present, the deadline for this course is 30 June 2027. A few months before that final deadline, the team re-evaluates the course’s continued relevance, and may decide to extend the deadline by a further six or twelve months. For now though, best to assume the final deadline is 30 June 2027.

The course’s current term began on 1 January 2026 and ends at that deadline. If the course is extended, a new term begins right after the deadline, and some of your work may carry forward into it, as [explained below](https://cs50.harvard.edu/x/faqs/#will-my-work-carry-forward-if-the-course-is-extended).

We cannot make any exceptions to the deadlines for any reason. We therefore encourage you, if possible, to avoid trying to rush to complete the course at the deadline and instead spread your studies out over a reasonable stretch of time.

<a id="will-my-work-carry-forward-if-the-course-is-extended"></a>

##### Will my work carry forward if the course is extended?

Yes, if you submitted at least one assignment during the current term, which began on 1 January 2026. In that case, every assignment you completed1 during the current term carries forward into the next term. Work submitted before the current term began does not carry forward.

If the course is updated to a new edition, you may then submit only the new edition’s assignments, and you will need to satisfy whatever requirements are in the course at that time. Work from prior editions of the course may not be submitted for credit.

1 By *completed*, we mean you earned a score of 70% or better on the assignment before the deadline. For course weeks that include multiple problems, you must complete all those problems for that week for the week to be considered complete and for those scores to carry forward.

<a id="obtaining-your-certificate"></a>

#### Obtaining Your Certificate

<a id="how-do-i-get-my-free-cs50-certificate"></a>

##### How do I get my free CS50 Certificate?

Once you have completed the course and met all of the requirements (per the above), you’ll see a link on your [Gradebook](https://cs50.me/cs50x) page allowing you to request a free CS50 Certificate, much like the one [shown here](https://cs50.harvard.edu/x/certificate/). This link will be available within approximately four hours after you receive your score for your last required assignment and your progress bar in your [Gradebook](https://cs50.me/cs50x) indicates course completion. Once this link appears, you should then proceed to log in using your **GitHub** credentials. If you have trouble accessing it, try logging out of GitHub and back in, then revisit the link to download your certificate. Be sure to download your free certificate **before** the deadline.

<a id="how-do-i-get-my-verified-certificate-from-edx"></a>

##### How do I get my verified certificate from edX?

Every three weeks, CS50’s staff provides edX with a list of students who have satisfied the course’s requirements (by receiving scores of at least 70% on each of the required problems). Typically, within a week thereafter, edX then generates students’ verified certificates (for students who have active verification), at which point they should appear automatically on edX’s [dashboard](https://courses.edx.org/dashboard). Thus, after you have satisfied the course’s requirements, it can take up to 30 days for your verified certificate to be generated by edX. Note that edX will **not** notify you, so be sure to check your dashboard periodically.

If it has been **more than 30 days** since you met all requirements and you have still not received your certificate despite holding an active verification that whole time, email certificates@cs50.harvard.edu with your details. Please do not email sooner; we cannot accept requests for expedited processing of verified certificates.

<a id="can-you-rush-the-issuance-of-my-verified-certificate-from-edx"></a>

##### Can you rush the issuance of my verified certificate from edX?

No.

<a id="why-did-i-not-get-my-verified-certificate-after-30-days"></a>

##### Why did I not get my verified certificate after 30 days?

First, recall that your verified edX certificate will be issued by edX up to 30 days after three criteria are met: (a) your [Gradebook](https://cs50.me/cs50x) shows that you have completed all of the required assignments with at least a 70% score or higher on each, (b) you have made payment to edX and completed their verification process, resulting in a verified badge on your edX dashboard, and (c) you have linked your [Gradebook](https://cs50.me/cs50x) to your edX account. Thus, you should ensure that 30 days have passed since all three of these above criteria have occurred.

Second, we can only inform edX of your passing status if you have linked your [Gradebook](https://cs50.me/cs50x) to edX. Visit your [Gradebook](https://cs50.me/cs50x) and ensure that no gold bar appears at the top of the page stating that you must link your [Gradebook](https://cs50.me/cs50x) to your edX account. If you see such a gold bar, complete the linking process. Then, re-read the above about the timing in which you might expect to receive your certificate.

Finally, if you still have not received your edX certificate, best to ensure that the edX username that appears at the top of your [Gradebook](https://cs50.me/cs50x) is the same edX username that appears at the top of your [edX dashboard](https://courses.edx.org/dashboard). If there is a mismatch between these usernames, you will need to inform edX that they need to transfer your verification payment to the correct username, as we have likely already informed them that the edX username that appears on top of your [Gradebook](https://cs50.me/cs50x) has passed the course.

<a id="what-name-will-appear-on-my-certificate"></a>

##### What name will appear on my certificate?

Free certificates will include the name you provide when you claim your certificate. You will personally type the name you wish to appear on your certificate during the redemption process. Make sure you type the name you desire to appear on your certificate!

Verified certificates provided through edX will include the name associated with your edX account.

<a id="can-i-change-the-name-on-my-certificate"></a>

##### Can I change the name on my certificate?

No, we cannot make changes to the name on your certificate. Because we rely upon you to type the name correctly and because of the huge number of students with whom we work, it is not feasible for us to provide name changes. You are welcome to upgrade to a verified certificate and communicate with edX about what name will appear on your verified edX certificate by emailing them at [info@edX.org](mailto:info@edx.org).
